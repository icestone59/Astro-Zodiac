# 5. P01 — Procrastination

## Construct
Trait/general procrastination

## Research Instruments

### General Procrastination Scale (GPS)
Developer:
C. H. Lay

Year:
1986

The GPS is widely used for general trait procrastination.

### GPS-9
Development and Validation:
General Procrastination Scale (GPS-9)

Year:
2019

The GPS-9 was reduced to 9 items across two large samples, and a later meta-analytic reliability estimate reported average alpha = 0.89 across 15 samples (total N = 4,492).

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886919302077

### Pure Procrastination Scale (PPS)
Steel (2010)

The PPS contains 12 items drawn from established procrastination measures and is used as a measure of general procrastination.

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5005418/

### Product Rule

Do not copy full item sets without permission.

MVP:
- use construct-informed custom questions
- cite GPS/GPS-9/PPS as research background
- do separate permission review before using original items

## Intervention
- Implementation intentions
- Small-step planning
- Behavioral activation principles

Implementation intentions have substantial research support for helping people translate goals into action.

# 6. P02 — Perfectionism

## Construct
Perfectionistic striving / evaluative concerns

### Primary Reference
Frost Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale (FMPS)

Frost et al., 1990.

Important dimensions include:
- Concern over Mistakes
- Doubts about Actions
- Personal Standards
- Organization
- Parental Expectations/Criticism

### FMPS-Brief

Burgess, Frost & DiBartolo, 2016

The 8-item brief version was evaluated with confirmatory factor analyses across community and clinical samples, with evidence supporting two dimensions: striving and evaluative concerns.

Source:
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0734282916651359

A later psychometric study also found a two-factor model for FMPS-Brief, while noting prior instability in factor structures.

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7426438/

## Intervention
- CBT-informed perfectionism exercises
- Behavioral experiments
- Reframing standards

Do not label the user “unhealthy perfectionist” from Astrology alone.

# 7. P03 — Decision Avoidance / Indecisiveness

## Construct
Indecisiveness / avoidant decision-making

### Indecisiveness Scale
Frost & Shows

Research examined relationships between indecisiveness, procrastination, perfectionistic evaluative concerns and decision-making latency.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/000579679390121A

### General Decision-Making Style

Scott & Bruce, 1995

The study developed scales for rational, avoidant, intuitive, dependent and spontaneous decision styles. Reported internal consistency coefficients ranged from .68 to .94 across the scales in the development work.

Source:
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0013164495055017
(If the exact DOI URL changes, retain the citation metadata in the database.)

## Intervention
- Decision criteria
- Time-boxed decisions
- Behavioral experiments
- Small reversible choices

# 8. P04 — Fear of Failure

## Evidence Position

Do not select one scale for MVP without additional instrument review.

Reason:
“Fear of failure” can refer to different constructs, including performance-related fear, shame, evaluation concerns and avoidance.

## MVP Approach

Use custom behavioral questions:

- ฉันหลีกเลี่ยงงานที่มีโอกาสผิดพลาดหรือไม่?
- ฉันมักเลือกทางที่ปลอดภัยเพราะกลัวล้มเหลวหรือไม่?
- ครั้งล่าสุดที่ความกลัวทำให้ฉันไม่ลงมือคืออะไร?

## Intervention
- Behavioral experiment
- Graded challenge
- CBT-informed thought restructuring

Important:
Do not present this as treatment for anxiety disorders.

# 9. P05 — Self-Efficacy

## General Self-Efficacy Scale (GSE)

Schwarzer & Jerusalem, 1995.

Objective:
Assess general perceived self-efficacy.

The instrument is described as a 10-item measure and is listed as public domain by ePROVIDE/MAPI Trust.

Source:
https://eprovide.mapi-trust.org/instruments/general-self-efficacy-scale

A Johns Hopkins resource reports internal consistency estimates across the original samples from .82 to .93.

Source:
https://cih.jhu.edu/measurement/general-self-efficacy-scale/

APA PsycTests DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1037/t00393-000

## Product Rule

Even for public-domain instruments, preserve the original scoring/meaning and cite the original source when used.

## Intervention
- Mastery experiences
- Small progressive goals
- Evidence-of-capability journal

# 10. P06 — Values / Direction

## Valuing Questionnaire (VQ)

Smout, Davies, Burns & Christie, 2014.

The VQ was developed within an ACT framework. Factor analyses supported two factors:
- Progress
- Obstruction

Development studies included undergraduate and clinical adult samples and examined concurrent validity with several established measures.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212144714000532

Additional source:
https://contextualscience.org/publications/development_valuing_questionnaire_vq

## Intervention
- Values clarification
- Committed action

## Product Rule
Use values work for self-development. Do not infer psychiatric conditions from low values scores.

# 18. Question Construction Rules

Questions in Astro-Zodiac must:

1. Ask about behavior or experience, not astrology belief.
2. Avoid leading language.
3. Avoid double-barreled questions.
4. Use a clear recall period where appropriate.
5. Be understandable without psychology knowledge.
6. Avoid shame or moral judgment.
7. Be culturally appropriate for Thai users.
8. Not imply diagnosis.
9. Not claim validity unless validated.
10. Have an identifiable source tier.

Example:

BAD:
> “เพราะ Saturn ของคุณทำให้คุณกลัวความล้มเหลวใช่ไหม?”

GOOD:
> “เมื่อคุณคิดว่ามีโอกาสทำพลาด คุณมักเลื่อนการลงมือหรือไม่?”
